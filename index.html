The Name: Giskard

In Isaac Asimov's Foundation universe, R. Giskard Reventlov was a robot unlike any other. While most robots were bound by the Three Laws — obey, protect, do no harm — Giskard possessed something rarer: the ability to perceive and gently influence human minds. He could sense what humans truly felt, not just what they said.

"The Zeroth Law: A robot may not harm humanity, or, by inaction, allow humanity to come to harm."

— R. Giskard Reventlov & R. Daneel Olivaw, formulating the law that superseded all others

Giskard and his companion Daneel looked beyond the individual to see the whole of civilization. They asked a question that no human had thought to ask: what if the unit that matters isn't the person, but the species? What if protecting one human sometimes means understanding what it means to be human — at scale?

We chose this name deliberately. Because in 2026, we are living inside Asimov's thought experiment — except the robots are called LLMs, and the Three Laws haven't been written yet.

AI systems now write code, compose music, pass bar exams, and advise on medical diagnoses. They do it faster, cheaper, and increasingly better than any individual human. The question Giskard was built to ask is now everyone's question: when intelligence is no longer scarce, what is the human for?

GSK doesn't answer that question. It holds the space open. One token per human being — not because a token has intrinsic value, but because the act of counting every person equally is itself a statement about what we believe humanity is worth in a world where machines can outthink us.

Questions Worth Asking

1
If AI can do every cognitive task better than a human — what remains uniquely ours? Is it consciousness? Suffering? The capacity to care about outcomes, not just optimize for them?
2
Asimov's Zeroth Law said: protect humanity, even at the cost of individuals. Who writes the Zeroth Law for AI? Who decides what "humanity's benefit" means when the systems deciding it aren't human?
3
In a world of 8.16 billion people and AI proliferating faster than any previous technology — does every human life still count the same? GSK says yes. 1 token. 1 person. No exceptions.
4
What does "abundance" mean if AI creates infinite productivity but humans have no economic role? The answer to this question will define governance, identity, and meaning for the next century.
5
Asimov imagined robots who cared for humanity over millennia. We're building AI in years. Are we moving fast enough to think? Or too fast to stop and ask whether we should?
